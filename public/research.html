Is Brain.fm legit?

Yes, it's a real company and not a scam. But the evidence is modest and the marketing runs ahead of it.

  • Company: Brain.fm, Inc., Brooklyn. The method dates to a 2005 patent (US7674224). It got a $225k US National Science Foundation small-business grant in 2017.
  • Price: $14.99/month or $99.99/year. No free tier, just a 7-day trial that needs a card and auto-renews. Refunds are only for EU residents within 14 days if you haven't streamed.
  • Reviews: Trustpilot 2.6/5, split between love and billing complaints (charged after cancelling, surprise annual charge). A mild dark pattern, not fraud.
  • The study (Woods et al., 2024, Communications Biology): 4 experiments, 5-minute lab sessions. The ADHD-related benefit is a small, borderline effect in people who self-report symptoms, not diagnosed ADHD. The first author works at Brain.fm and holds equity, and the music was theirs. No independent replication yet.
  • What they do: pulse the volume of the 200 Hz–1 kHz band of music at about 16 Hz. In their test, 16 Hz beat 8 and 32 Hz. Pulse depth made no significant difference.

How to use it

  • Choose sound or silence. Every sound is optional. Switch it off whenever you prefer.
  • Keep it at conversation level (about 60–70 dB): the lowest level that covers the room. The studies used 70–80 dB for minutes; you'll use it for hours.
  • No lyrics during reading or writing. Words in music hurt reading and verbal memory. Instrumental lofi is roughly neutral.
  • Use it in blocks, not all day. All studies were short sessions. Nobody knows if the effect fades as you get used to it, so the Pomodoro breaks help here too.
  • If a sound distracts you, stop. A timer or sound preference does not measure attention or brain arousal.
